#! nostage
# Varda: a portal you built pins itself, only once, and one built beside it gets a pin of its own.
#
# Stand somewhere with no pins of your own within a dozen metres before running this. The
# first line asserts an empty map nearby, and a base you have already marked by hand will
# fail it for a reason that is nothing to do with the mod.
#
# The DUNGEON half is varda-pins-a-dungeon, which places a real crypt and walks through its
# door. This covers the portal half, which is the one that runs off the portal's own heartbeat
# and is therefore the one most likely to run twice.
#
# Both portals are left untagged, because no step can type a tag. What a row of TAGGED portals
# does is covered by hand at the top of varda-forgets-a-destroyed-portal.

# Standing still first, on the surface. The dungeon scenario runs just before this one and ends
# by walking back out of its crypt and then going back to where it began, a hundred metres from
# the crypt. It used to end at the crypt's door, and on 2026-09-29 the first `place` below met the
# crypt's wall. On 2026-09-28 this file began while the walk out of the crypt was
# still in the air: the first portal went down at y 5033, inside the crypt (Player.log has
# "placed at world height 5033,01" two seconds before "Setting forced environment" went
# blank), and the player landed on the surface five metres from it in plan. Both pins were
# right, each on its own portal, and `pins 1 4` found neither, because the portal it meant was
# never three metres away. The dungeon file ended on `landed` that day, but Devkit then started
# the next scenario the moment the last step returned, and dropped a block set by a LAST step at
# that handover (fixed in Devkit 1b48353). The dungeon file now has a line after it as well, and
# this one waits for the move itself, because a scenario run on its own or after something else
# cannot lean on either. When nothing is in flight it answers "ready" at once.
landed

# The ground made ready here rather than before the first step, which the `#! nostage` line at
# the top turns off. Devkit's own stage runs before `landed`, so with a walk out of the crypt
# still in flight it would clear and level wherever the player happened to be, or find them in
# the crypt and do nothing, and the surface this file builds on would be left as it was.
stage

clear
god
ghost
flatten

# A known map. Without this the scenario inherits whatever ran before it - the dungeon one
# leaves a Burial Chambers pin behind, and this opened by asserting an empty map and finding
# it. DELETES saved pins within 40m, hand-placed ones included.
unpin 40

# Nothing of ours here yet, at the radius every line below uses. Saved pins only, so the
# player marker and a bed do not count.
pins 0 12

# `place` goes through Player.PlacePiece, which writes the creator id - and the creator is
# exactly what Varda checks, because pinning every portal the world streams past you would put
# other people's network on your map.
place portal_wood
wait 3

pins 1 12

# The interesting question is not whether the heartbeat pins but whether it stops. It runs
# twice a second for as long as the portal is loaded, so if the portal did not find its own pin
# on its own spot this would be eight pins by now.
wait 4
pins 1 12

# A second portal five metres to the side of the first, the way portals stand at a hub, gets a
# pin of its own. The first version gave the two of them one pin between them, because anything
# inside the 8m MergeRadius was taken for the pin already there, and with two different tags
# that one pin changed its name twice a second.
#
# `place` puts the piece in front of you and the player does not move here, so the first portal
# is three metres ahead and the second is nearly six away, diagonally. Measure from where the
# player stands, not from where the pieces are: that was the whole lesson of this file's first
# run, which put a radius exactly on a portal and counted it in.
place portal_wood 3 5
wait 3

pins 2 12

# Each pin on its own portal, not both on the first. Only the first portal is inside four metres
# of the player, so only one pin may be.
pins 1 4

# And they stay two. Ten more heartbeats each, in which neither may take the other's pin for its
# own, add a third, or drop one.
wait 5
pins 2 12
pins 1 4
